\begin{block}{Motivation}

In \textbf{high-stakes settings} like scientific discovery and medical diagnosis, AI agents must \textbf{ask smart questions} before acting. People are remarkably efficient information-seekers, balancing \textbf{exploration} and \textbf{action} under uncertainty. Can we build LM agents that do the same?

\end{block}

\begin{block}{Collaborative Battleship}

We adapt a classic task paradigm from cognitive science [1--4] into an evaluation harness for AI agents. The \textbf{Captain} must balance \emph{exploration} (asking questions) and \emph{action} (taking shots), while the \textbf{Spotter} must supply accurate, contextually-grounded answers.

\begin{block}{Method: Bayesian Experimental Design}

We formalize question selection as BED [5--7]. Given hidden state $S$, we compute the utility of question $x$ as the expected information gain (EIG) after observing answer $y$:
%
\begin{center}
\begin{minipage}[c]{0.38\colwidth}
\centering
$\displaystyle p(S \mid x, y) \;\propto\; p(y \mid S, x) \;\; p(S)$
\end{minipage}\hfill
\begin{minipage}[c]{0.58\colwidth}
\colorbox{AirBlue!15}{\;\;$\displaystyle\operatorname{EIG}(x) = H\big[p(S)\big] - \mathbb{E}_{y \sim p(y \mid x)}\!\Big[H\big[p(S \mid x, y)\big]\Big]$\;\;}
\end{minipage}
\end{center}

{\bfseries\textcolor{HonoluluBlue}{Three inference-time strategies.}}\enspace Computing EIG over combinatorial hypotheses $s \in \mathcal{S}$ and experiments $x \in \mathcal{X}$ is intractable; we approximate via \emph{Monte Carlo sampling} of both world states and questions.

At each turn $t$, the Captain maintains a belief $p_t(s)$. Let $p^{\mathrm{hit}}_t(u) = \sum_{s} \mathds{1}[\text{ship at } u \text{ in } s]\, p_t(s)$.

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabular*}{\colwidth}{l@{\extracolsep{\fill}}lll}
  \toprule
  \textbf{Strategy} & \textbf{Action} & \textbf{Rule} & \textbf{Intuition} \\
  \midrule
  $Q_{\mathrm{Bayes}}$ & Ask & $x_t^{\star} = \arg\max_{x} \operatorname{EIG}(x);\; x \sim p_{\text{LM}}(x)$ & Sample and score questions by EIG \\
  $M_{\mathrm{Bayes}}$ & Shoot & $u_t^{\star} = \arg\max_{u}\, p^{\mathrm{hit}}_t(u)$ & Highest-probability (MAP) action \\
  $D_{\mathrm{Bayes}}$ & Decide & Ask if $\gamma\, p^{\mathrm{hit}}_{t+1}(u_{t+1}^{\star} \mid x_t^{\star}) > p^{\mathrm{hit}}_t(u_t^{\star})$ & One-step lookahead with discount $\gamma$ \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular*}
\end{center}

def answer(board, obs):
  # Find orange ship
  orange = np.argwhere(board == 4)
  # What does the captain see?
  found = set(obs[obs > 0])
  # Check neighbors for another ship
  for x, y in orange:
    for dx, dy in neighbors(x, y):
      ship = board[dx, dy]
      if (ship > 0 and
          ship not in found):
        return True
  return False
