\begin{block}{Motivation}

In settings like \textbf{scientific discovery} and \textbf{medical diagnosis}, AI agents must \emph{ask smart questions} before acting---but current LMs are optimized to \emph{answer} questions, not \emph{ask} good ones.

\end{block}

\begin{block}{Collaborative Battleship}

\begin{center}
  \includegraphics[width=0.95\colwidth]{figures/battleship-splash-2.png}
\end{center}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Captain}: asks yes/no questions, then fires at tiles
  \item \textbf{Spotter}: sees full board, provides grounded answers
  \item 8$\times$8 board, 4 ships, budget of 15 questions + 40 shots
\end{itemize}

\end{block}

\begin{block}{Method: Bayesian Experimental Design}

We formalize question selection as BED. Given hidden state $S$, experiment $x$, observation $y$:
%
\begin{equation*}
  \underbrace{p(S \mid x, y)}_{\text{posterior}}
  \;\propto\;
  \underbrace{p(y \mid S, x)}_{\text{likelihood}}
  \;\;
  \underbrace{p(S)}_{\text{prior}}
\end{equation*}

Select questions to maximize \textbf{Expected Information Gain}:
%
\begin{center}
\colorbox{AirBlue!15}{\;\;$\displaystyle\operatorname{EIG}(x) = \mathbb{E}_{y \sim p(y \mid x)}\!\Big[H\big[p(S)\big] - H\big[p(S \mid x, y)\big]\Big]$\;\;}
\end{center}

For binary answers with noisy oracle ($\mathrm{BSC}(\varepsilon)$):
$\operatorname{EIG}_\varepsilon = H_b\!\big(\varepsilon + (1 - 2\varepsilon)\,P(y\!=\!1)\big) - H_b(\varepsilon)$, maximized when $P(y\!=\!1) \approx 0.5$.

\heading{Three inference-time strategies}

At each turn $t$, the Captain maintains a belief $p_t(S)$ over hidden boards. Let $p^{\mathrm{hit}}_t(u) = \Pr(\text{ship at } u \mid p_t)$.

\vspace{-0.5ex}

\begin{center}
\renewcommand{\arraystretch}{1.4}
\begin{tabular*}{\colwidth}{l@{\extracolsep{\fill}}lll}
  \toprule
  \textbf{Strategy} & \textbf{Action} & \textbf{Rule} & \textbf{Intuition} \\
  \midrule
  $Q_{\mathrm{Bayes}}$ & Ask & $x_t^{\star} = \arg\max_{x} \operatorname{EIG}_\varepsilon(x);\; x \sim p_{\text{LM}}(x)$ & Sample and score questions by EIG \\
  $M_{\mathrm{Bayes}}$ & Shoot & $u_t^{\star} = \arg\max_{u}\, p^{\mathrm{hit}}_t(u)$ & Highest-probability (MAP) tile \\
  $D_{\mathrm{Bayes}}$ & Decide & Ask if $\gamma\, \max_u p^{\mathrm{hit}}_{t+1}(u \mid x_t^{\star}) > \max_u p^{\mathrm{hit}}_t(u)$ & One-step lookahead \\
  \bottomrule
\end{tabular*}
\end{center}

\end{block}

  \colorbox{HonoluluBlue!15}{\makebox[\linewidth][l]{\small\strut\;\textbf{Captain:} ``Is there another ship near yellow?''}}

  \vspace{1pt}

  \begin{tcolorbox}[colback=MonokaiBG, colframe=MonokaiBG, boxrule=0pt, arc=2pt, left=4pt, right=4pt, top=4pt, bottom=4pt]
  \begin{lstlisting}[style=pythondark]
def answer(board, partial):
  found = set(partial[partial > 0])          # already sunk
  yellow = np.argwhere(board == 4)           # "yellow" ship
  for x, y in yellow:
    for dx, dy in neighbors(x, y):           # within 1 tile
      ship = board[dx, dy]
      if ship > 0 and ship not in found:     # "another"
        return True
  return False
  \end{lstlisting}
  \end{tcolorbox}

  \vspace{1pt}

  \colorbox{SeaGreen!15}{\makebox[\linewidth][l]{\small\strut\;\textbf{Spotter:} \textcolor{SeaGreen}{\checkmark} \textbf{Yes}}}

\begin{resultbox}
  With Bayesian strategies, our approach boosts \textbf{Llama-4-Scout} from random chance to expert-level:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item[\textbullet] \textbf{Better guessing:} Targeting improves from \textbf{0.37 F1} $\rightarrow$ \textbf{0.76 F1}
    \item[\textbullet] \textbf{Super-human:} Win rate vs. humans \textbf{8\%} $\rightarrow$ \textbf{82\%}
    \item[\textbullet] \textbf{Surpasses frontier:} Win rate vs. GPT-5 \textbf{0\%} $\rightarrow$ \textbf{67\%}, at \textbf{100$\times$} lower cost
  \end{itemize}
\end{resultbox}
