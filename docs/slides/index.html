Shoot First, Ask Questions Later?

Building Rational Agents That Explore and Act Like People

Motivation

The information-seeking gap

  • Problem: High-stakes AI applications (science, diagnosis) require agents that ask smart questions before acting — yet we lack benchmarks for explore/exploit tradeoffs.
  • Our benchmark: Collaborative Battleship — a two-player dialogue task where a Captain balances asking questions (exploration) with taking shots (exploitation).
  • Behavior gap: LM agents ask less informative questions, give inaccurate answers, and waste turns on low-probability moves compared to human players (N=42).
  • Our approach: Monte Carlo inference strategies based on Bayesian Experimental Design (BED) that guide what to ask, where to shoot, and when to explore vs. act.

 Optimizing Questions via Expected Information Gain

Method

Bayesian strategies for rational agents

Belief-aware policies help agents decide what to ask, where to shoot, and when to explore vs. act.

Precision Moves

Bayes-M

Maintain a weighted particle belief over feasible boards and fire at the tile with the highest posterior probability of containing a ship.

InfoMax Questions

Bayes-Q

Sample candidate questions, translate to executable checks, and choose the one whose expected information gain (EIG) is highest under the current belief.

Explore vs. Act

Bayes-D

Compare post-question MAP hit rate against the current best shot; ask when the value of information exceeds immediate reward.
